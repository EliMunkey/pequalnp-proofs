# Independent audit of the final 18-gate search

**Scope.** I audited the physical AND/OR search with the free complemented input fixed at vertex zero, as run in `actual/ordered18.log`. Its recorded verdict is UNSAT after 25,582,855 visited states, 1,082,835 memo hits, and 319,660 distinct boundary functions. The prerequisite unrestricted signed exclusion through 17 gates and the lower bounds supplied for restricted functions are separate computations. The final DFS is not kernel-certified.

**Boundary compression.** With the zeroth input fixed, each signal is monotone in the other nine inputs. The 38 selected rows comprise every minimal true and maximal false assignment in each slice. Every positive row lies above a selected positive in its own slice and every negative row lies below a selected negative. Equality on these rows therefore suffices at the *output*. When an intermediate wire agrees on all 38 rows with an available physical rail or a constant, replacing it preserves final boundary values and the slice monotonicity of the resulting circuit. The replacement consequently preserves the entire target, contradicting the signed exclusion through 17. This argument does not require intermediate functions agreeing on 38 rows to agree globally.

**Scheduling and symmetry.** The 12 automorphisms fixing zero partition 45 unordered pairs into eight orbits. The first gate may be moved to its orbit's least pair; the second gate starts a newly ordered suffix. For this suffix greedily schedule an eligible gate with smallest operand pair in the lexicographic order (larger wire place, smaller wire place). Every freshly eligible gate uses the newly assigned largest place, so its key exceeds that of the gate just scheduled. An optimum ordering passing the numeric threshold exists. All gate places are below 32, so the integer order is exact.

**Memoization.** Sorted keys retain each live boundary function, physical polarity availability, wire place, and the previously scheduled pair. The newest place identifies the depth. Thus two equal states permit the same physically legal, order-respecting boundary continuations. If a continuation computed the target after one prefix, the same continuation after the other would also agree on the boundary and hence globally. This justifies merging histories even when their pin propagation and specialized ancestor cones differ. A capped memo merely ceases to store additional states. Full C++ keys resolve hash collisions.

**Separation and specialized cones.** For any positive/negative boundary pair, a monotone continuation needs some available rail which is true on the positive and false on the negative. Both signs count while the zeroth signal survives. A gate removes the last separating rail exactly when it retires a unique separator or retires both members of a two-way tie. In these cases its new physical output must separate the pair. Padding bits remain identical on both sides. Under a pin, absorption removes both operand cones, a neutral constant selects the other operand's cone up to sign, and two unknown operands retain both cones plus the current gate. The union over live signals gives an upper bound on the number of signed gates required to reproduce them. A nonconstant residual must eliminate at least one future gate for each currently live constant. The comparison to the independently supplied restricted lower bound therefore has the claimed direction.

**Independent verification.** Lean checks the exact 38 masks and coverage of all 1,024 rows, as well as monotonicity by induction on the physically permitted circuit. The formal theorem is `PhysicalBoundary.physical_compressed_iff` in `PetersenPhysicalBoundary.lean`. An independent direct physical truth-table enumerator agreed with the final ordered DFS on 522 small instances (336 negative and 186 positive), both without minor pruning and with all one- and two-variable cofactor bounds independently regenerated. The second fixture exercises the actual pin and backward-cone pruning.
